Clinical–Computational Methods Research Programme

Seven pre-registered studies asking one plain question: which Western methods still work when the patients, labs, and clinic routines look different — especially in India? What happens when we move a model from US to Indian clinics?

Main idea: One engineering run, two papers: (A) how far can US data be stretched toward rural India before the model breaks, and (B) how robust are trial-style effect estimates when most Indian patients use non-prescription / AYUSH medicines.
  • 005 Transport: As we tilt source data G0→G3 (BMI 28.3→22.8, Thin-fat 0→56.7%, generic 100→4.7%) — at which grade does data overlap collapse and re-tuning become required?

Figure 1 — How far can we stretch US data before the model breaks?

Grades G0 (US-like) → G3 (rural India–like). Two curves: how confident the model looks (AUC) vs how much usable overlap remains (ESS). Translated: “model confidence rises as populations diverge — then collapses.”

↳ At G2 the model needs re-tuning — before this, overlap is too thin
Plain read: By G2 (AUC 0.91, but only 1.7% effective overlap, 38% data trimmed) you can still publish a dose–response, but deploying without re-tuning would be misleading. At G3 (AUC 0.97, 0.5% overlap, 67% trimmed) you must re-tune — the US data no longer covers the Indian clinic. Technical gloss
Pilot 5k SE ±0.015 → Full 40k SE ±0.010 (tighter). G0 BMI 28.3, MONO 0%, HSC 78%, generic 100%; G3 BMI 22.8, MONO 56.7%, HSC 15%, generic 4.7%, AYUSH 0→96%.

Figure 2 — Diabetes labels travel, but groupings don’t

8k British South Asian proxy: European 5-cluster centroids vs fresh de-novo clustering. Completeness = everyone gets a label. ARI = do labels agree?

98.4% get a label (≥85% target ✓) ARI 0.25 agreement (≥0.60 target ✗) 3-test ARI 0.45 · silhouette 0.11/0.17 poor
Plain read: You can force every patient into a European bucket — but the buckets don’t mean the same thing in South Asian bodies (thin-fat mismatch). A simpler 3-test version (age, BMI, HbA1c — dropping GADA/HOMA) does slightly better and needs to be the co-primary test. CARRS will confirm if 6→3 ablation still holds at GADA <10%.

Figure 3 — Do papers report calibration the same way before and after 2024?

Corpus audit 570 TRIPOD validations → 150 target. Primary outcome is interval-aware reporting — not “slope mentioned” but “slope with uncertainty (CI or band).”

κ trajectory: 0.615 (n=40) → 0.576 (n=90) → 0.545 (n=150) Wilson primary: 47/150 = 0.313 [0.245, 0.391]

κ = inter-rater agreement (Landis&Koch: ≥0.60 moderate, ≥0.70 substantial). Wilson stable at ~30% — no inflation as n grew (0.275→0.283→0.300→0.313 within CI per Riley).

Plain-language glossary — hover terms on the cards

Methods wording is here once, so cards stay readable. Each term keeps the paper’s number but explains it as you’d tell a colleague on the ward.

Data overlap shrinks ESS/n
After re-weighting to match the Indian target, how much of the US data is still usable. 1.00→0.005 means 100% → 0.5% remains — the rest is trimmed as non-overlapping.
Methods: effective sample size / N · ESS
Model confidence drops calibration slope / ICI
A model can rank correctly (AUC) but be over-confident — e.g. predicts 40% risk where truth is 18%. Slope <0.8 or ICI >0.02 flags this.
Methods: calibration slope & integrated calibration index
Data trimming trim 10%
% of patients dropped because they have no close match in the target population at that grade. 67% at G3 means two-thirds fall outside the overlap zone.
Methods: propensity trimming
Model needs re-tuning transport vs recalibration
When overlap is too thin, you can’t just re-weight — you must refit. Threshold used here: ESS/n <0.02 and trim >~5% at G2.
Methods: S-admissibility diagnostics
Groups agree? ARI
Adjusted Rand Index: do two ways of grouping patients agree? 0 = random, 1 = perfect. 0.25 on SA proxy is “labels exist, meaning shifted.”
Methods: ARI ≥0.60 transports · target 0.40 for 3-var
Do papers show uncertainty? interval-aware + Wilson
Not “did they mention slope?” but “did they show the interval/band around it?” Wilson is the uncertainty on that proportion (31% [24%,39%]).
Methods: Riley 2024 + Wilson interval
Hidden confounding needed to flip result R*
How strong an unmeasured factor (e.g. AYUSH use 10–96%) must be to erase an observed RR of 1.2 or 1.8. R* 1.0–1.5 = fragile; 1.8+ = robust.
Methods: E-value / bias factor R*
Reviewer agreement kappa κ
Do two independent screeners agree beyond chance? κ 0.55–0.61 is moderate; ≥0.70 substantial. Trajectory 0.615→0.545 triggers re-train.
Methods: Cohen’s κ · Po/Pe
