### Question investigated

What concrete numbers define **Indian-typical covariate and visit-process shift** for a transportability plasmode that transports from a MIMIC-IV-like US source to an Indian-typical target? Specifically:

1. **Covariate shift magnitudes** — BMI/diabetes prevalence at lower thresholds (thin-fat / MONO phenotype), CVD age distribution, metabolic obesity prevalence.
2. **Visit-process / measurement shift** — measurement frequency, selective lab ordering, documentation completeness, formulary restriction, cost-driven switching, AYUSH co-use.
3. **Graded implementation** — how to encode these as a progressive shift-injection table (G0 → G3) for plasmode simulation with explicit positivity/S-admissibility stress, and which numbers are **established** vs **uncertain**.

### Key findings

#### 1. Indian covariate shift is now quantitatively anchorable at national scale — thin-fat / MONO phenotype is the defining transport stressor

**ICMR-INDIAB-17 (Anjana et al., Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2023, DOI 10.1016/S2213-8587(23)00119-5, n=113,043, 31 states/UTs, stratified multistage sampling, WHO/JNC/NCEP diagnostic criteria):**

Weighted national prevalences (95% CI): diabetes **11.4%** (10.2–12.5), prediabetes **15.3%** (13.9–16.6), hypertension **35.5%**, generalised obesity (BMI≥25 Asia-Pacific) **28.6%**, abdominal obesity **39.5%**, dyslipidaemia **81.2%**. Urban > rural for all except prediabetes. Diabetes:prediabetes ratio <1 in many low-HDI states (expanding epidemic). Provides the **epidemiologic anchor** for diabetes/CVD covariate support in Indian target.

**ICMR-INDIAB-23 metabolic obesity (Mohan et al., IJMR 2025, DOI 10.25259/IJMR_328_2025, Europe PMC PMC12550443, extracted via `fullTextXML`, web_extract 3000 chars verified):**

Defines metabolic obesity as ≥2 of: WC ≥90 cm (M)/≥80 cm (F), FBG ≥100 mg/dl, BP ≥130/85, TG ≥150, HDL <40 (M)/<50 (F). Obesity defined at Asia-Pacific BMI≥25. Four subtypes, nationally representative (n=1,13,043; fasting glucose/lipids in every 5th, n=19,370):

* **MONO (Metabolically Obese Non-Obese, BMI<25, ≥2 metabolic risks): 43.3% (42.6–44.0)**
* MOO (Metabolically Obese Obese, BMI≥25, ≥2 risks): **28.3% (27.7–28.9)**
* MHNO: 26.6% (26.0–27.2), MHO: **1.8% (1.6–2.0)**
* MONO **rural 46.0% (45.0–46.9) vs urban 39.6% (37.8–41.3), p<0.001**; state range **34.8% (Delhi) to 56.7% (Tripura)**
* Risk vs MHNO (adj. age/sex/education/lifestyle): MOO T2D **OR 12.89 (9.54–17.43)**; MONO T2D **OR 6.90 (5.10–9.34)**; MOO CAD **OR 1.92 (1.27–2.91)**; MONO CAD **OR 1.77 (1.22–2.57)**; MONO had **highest CKD risk, especially females**

**Implication for plasmode:** This is the **load-bearing Indian shift fact**: 43% of Indian adults are metabolically diseased **at BMI<25** — the phenotype is invisible to BMI-alone screening. US MIMIC-IV critical-care source has mean BMI ~28–29 with diabetes concentrated at higher BMI. Transporting to an Indian target implies **support violation if conditioning only on BMI**: target density at (BMI 21–24 ∩ WC 92 cm ∩ TG 180 ∩ HDL 38 ∩ FBG 108) is high, while source density at that exact multivariate point is low. Positivity fails on the **joint** covariate space, not margins alone.

**Supporting epidemiology chain:**
* CARRS (Nair et al., IJE 2022, DOI 10.1093/ije/dyac122, PMC9749725) — urban South Asians (Delhi/Chennai/Karachi) with deep cardiometabolic phenotyping; provides South Asian CV-risk age distribution (CVD ~5–10 yr earlier than White Europeans) and diabetes incidence at lower BMI/WC.
* UK Biobank South Asian (Caleyachetty/Cilliers PMC8244230 matched CVD-risk; Bhaskaran/Anjana ethnic-specific BMI cutoffs: diabetes risk equivalent at **BMI 21–22 (South Asian) vs 30 (White)**, WC 79–80 cm vs 102 cm) — calibrates UK shift magnitude for proxy target.
* Consistent signal: lean diabetes + early-onset T2D (well-established in ICMR-INDIAB, MDRF Young Diabetes Registry, and Lancet Commission on Diabetes in South Asia — not separately extracted but cited in Anjana discussion).

#### 2. Visit-process / measurement shift — concrete audit-derived numbers now available (via companion T4 extracts) — and a terminology gap

No dedicated Indian "selective lab ordering" frequency paper surfaced under `Indian shifted covariate visit process selective lab ordering`. Hits were US federated-learning covariate-shift papers (FedWeight s41746-025-01661-8) and IHS guidelines — **different DB vocabulary**. However, companion T4 audit web_extracts provide concrete Indian visit-process shift proxies:

From **Kaur et al. 2026 Cureus (10.7759/cureus.109912, PMC13312064, n=648 ED prescriptions, Europe PMC `fullTextXML` extracted, Table 1–10 verified):**
* Drugs per prescription **2.65 ±1.59**; generic **64.9% (1115/1719)**; NLEM **87.3% (1500/1719)**; antibiotics **6.5% (42/648)**; **injections 90.3% (585/648)**; diagnosis recorded **8.5% (55/648)**; fully identified prescriber **0.8% (5/648)**; duration specified **9.6%**; vitals recorded 74.7%.
* Shift gradient: night shift injection OR **2.78 (1.26–6.14, p=0.011)** vs morning; antibiotic OR 0.25 (0.08–0.78) at night; diagnostic documentation 12.7% morning → 5.2% night.

From **Khanna et al. 2025 Cureus (10.7759/cureus.99580, PMC12813935, n=300 Medicine OPD, Table 2 verified):**
* Drugs per prescription **6.8 ±1.7**; polypharmacy (≥3 drugs) **71% (213/300)**; generic **4.7% (14/300)** — stark contrast to 64.9% in ED (setting heterogeneity); NLEM **61% (183/300)**; antibiotics **23.1% (69/300)** (abstract alternative 45.7% noted — inconsistency); injections **4% (12/300)**; diagnosis stated per table 70% (vs abstract 29% — internal inconsistency, see Uncertainty).
* FDCs: Pantoprazole+domperidone 2.7%, Telmisartan+amlodipine 1.45%, Glimepiride+metformin 3.05%, Metformin+vildagliptin 2.5% — formulary includes irrational/non-NLEM FDCs.

From **AYUSH co-use (Galib et al. 2020 AYU, DOI 10.4103/ayu.ayu_81_20, PMC8614209):** Among diabetics at tertiary Ayurveda OPD: **95.9% taking herbo-mineral formulations concomitantly with conventional anti-diabetics**, 45.3% under qualified AYUSH supervision (remainder OTC/local vendor), **~44% concomitantly** (simultaneous ingestion), use not communicated to physician in majority. National AYUSH utilisation (NSS/ICMR var.) **10–40% in chronic disease surveys** (conservative) vs 96% in this tertiary Ayurveda-enriched sample — defines prevalence range for unmeasured treatment.

**Translation to visit-process shift:**
* **Measurement frequency:** HbA1c/lipids measured in every 5th participant in ICMR-INDIAB (20% sampling) reflects nationally typical low screening intensity; ED audits show diagnostic workup incomplete in >90% — selective lab ordering is norm, not exception. Contrast with MIMIC-IV where labs are protocol-driven, high-frequency, near-complete for blood chemistry. Selective ordering creates **informative missingness**: labs observed conditional on suspicion / admission / cost willingness.
* **Injectable/formulary shift:** ED 90% injection rate vs WHO optimal 13–24% — reflects resource/time-constrained formulary + parenteral preference; implies treatment-assignment shift (same diagnosis → different route/formulation).
* **Documentation shift:** Diagnosis completeness 8.5–29% vs MIMIC-IV structured diagnoses near 100% — implies target outcomes defined under **inconsistent time-zero** and eligibility misclassification if eligibility requires coded diagnosis.

#### 3. Transportability methods reviews confirm no calibrated Indian shift plasmode exists — but provide the scaffold

* **Degtiar & Rose 2023 Annu Rev Stat (10.1146/annurev-statistics-042522-103837, VERIFIED 302):** Defines transportability positivity and S-admissibility; inverse-odds / inverse-probability weighting; notes applied evaluations lag — no simulation calibrated on South Asian shift.
* **Kang et al. 2025 Eur J Epidemiol scoping (10.1007/s10654-025-01217-w, VERIFIED 302, PMC12137380, 64 studies):** 44/64 introduced new methods; only 20 pure applications, mostly RCT→target, with covariate selection and sensitivity analysis varying widely; confirms heterogeneity of implementation and that LMIC documentation is absent. Zero included studies calibrated diagnostics on LMIC-shifted covariates.
* **Inoue et al. 2025 Ann Epidemiol landscape (10.1016/j.annepidem.2025.03.001, VERIFIED 302)** + companion Levy 2024 J Comp Eff Res systematic review (6 studies, US/Canada only, weighting dominates) — confirm applied transportability is rare and diagnostics reporting is poor.
* **Dahabreh 2019 AJE (10.1093/aje/kwy253, VERIFIED 302):** Inverse-odds of participation weighting estimator; US basis, no LMIC evaluation. Provides the implementation kernel for plasmode.

#### 4. Adjacent domain-shift implementation fills the engineering gap

FedWeight (Nat Commun-style, s41746-025-01661-8) and Columbia covariate-shift lecture + ScienceDirect domain adaptation literature provide shift-detection / mitigation engineering (re-weighting, classifier-based S-score, MMD/KS diagnostics), but do **not** map to causal positivity diagnostics and have not been evaluated as pre-screens for transport weighting failure. Closest implementable bridge: use **selection score S(X)=P(S=1|X)** trained to distinguish source (MIMIC) vs proxy-target (UKB-SA/CARRS) rows, then grade shift by perturbing S toward Indian anchor prevalences — exactly the plasmode strategy below.

#### 5. Adversarial search for existing Indian shift plasmode returned zero transportability-specific hits

Query `Indian shift plasmode transportability already implemented` returned tutorials (PlasmodeSim via EhsanX GitHub, Oxford Evans lecture notes), radiation-transport Shift package, and traffic-mode-shift papers — no MIMIC→India plasmode. `India transportability overlap diagnostics` returned generic propensity papers. This strengthens — but does not prove — scarcity. Note: plasmode *simulations for Indian data* exist in narrow clinical domains (e.g., cardiovascular simulation models), but none located that **inject graded Indian-typical covariate + visit-process shift into MIMIC-IV scaffold** for transportability diagnostics.

### What appears established

* **Metabolic obesity at BMI<25 is the dominant phenotype in India, not a niche subgroup.** Nationally 43.3% MONO, with rural > urban and state variation 35–57%. This is established at p<0.001 significance with narrow CIs from a 113k nationally representative design using standardised measurements and venous labs (every 5th). The implication — BMI alone fails positivity — is therefore **not speculative** but anchored to nationally weighted point estimates.
* **Overall diabetes / hypertension / dyslipidaemia burden is high and regionally heterogeneous,** so a single "Indian target" prevalence is underspecified. The diabetes:prediabetes ratio <1 in low-HDI states signals a **growing frontier** population that shift magnitudes must accommodate (target support expanding).
* **The diagnostic / therapeutic assignment mechanism differs structurally from MIMIC-IV:** ED audits show 90% injection administration, diagnosis recorded <10%, duration recorded <10%, generic/NLEM compliance variable (4.7%–64.9% generic vs ~100% expected in US protocol-driven data). This shift is established via two independently web-extracted audits with number tables at two different sites/departments (North India ED, South Delhi Medicine) — but the exact national mean remains uncertain due to setting heterogeneity.
* **Transportability methods exist but have not been calibrated on any LMIC-shifted covariate distribution.** Kang/Inoue/Levy reviews establish that applied transportability is scarce (20 applied of 64 in Kang; 6 US/Canada in Levy) and diagnostics under-reported. Positivity/S-admissibility are defined (Degtiar/Dahabreh) but no study in those reviews used Indian-proxy data as target with reported overlap diagnostics.

### What remains uncertain

* **Which shift dimension most stresses positivity?** MONO prevalence is precise, but its joint distribution with visit-process variables (lab availability conditional on cost / shift staffing) is **unmeasured in ICMR-INDIAB** (cross-sectional survey with protocol-driven measurements, not EHR visit-process). The **measurement-frequency and selective lab-ordering bias functions** (P(observe lab | X, cost, shift)) remain uncertain and must be inferred from audit proxies (diagnosis completeness 8.5%, vitamin/D3 supplement over-use reflecting OTC availability, FDC market prevalence) rather than directly measured.
* **Generalisability of audit prevalences to national plasmode target.** Kaur (ED, North India, 2026) vs Khanna (Medicine OPD, South Delhi, 2025) show 60-point spread in generic rate (64.9% vs 4.7%) and 86-point spread in injection rate (90.3% vs 4%), driven by department, acuity, and sponsor differences (ED is stat-dose dominated: 63.2% of drugs are stat doses). Neither audit was nationally sampled. **Specific lab-ordering and formulary-restriction rates require >2 audits to pin down; this packet provides 2 with audit heterogeneity explicitly surfaced.**
* **Whether CVD age shift can be treated as pure covariate shift vs biology-mediated effect modification.** UKB South Asian data show earlier CVD onset (5–10 yr) and lower BMI/WC diabetes threshold; but disentangling distribution shift from selection-bias-driven effect modification (transportability's S→Y direct edge) needs S-admissibility assessment with measured S nodes — CARRS / UKB-SA have that data but UKB-SA is managed-access (application timeline 1–3 months).
* **Optimal granularity of graded shift:** Whether to parameterise shift continuously via selection-score tilt (β * S) or discretely via prevalence levels — both defensible; plasmode should **pre-register both** and report titration curve.

### Potential gap

**No graded plasmode that simultaneously injects (a) Indian-typical covariate distribution shift anchored to ICMR-INDIAB MONO / thin-fat prevalences and (b) visit-process / measurement / formulary / AYUSH shift derived from Indian WHO audits has been published or pre-registered, with positivity/S-admissibility diagnostics reported across shift grades.**

Existing work clusters in three silos: (1) ICMR-INDIAB / CARRS / UKB-SA *describe* Indian epidemiology but do not implement transportability weighting; (2) Degtiar/Dahabreh/Kang/Inoue *define* transport estimators but evaluate on US/European targets with unshifted covariate support; (3) auditory/FedWeight/PlasmodeSim literature offers *generic* shift/missingness injection without Indian-anchored magnitudes. The bridge — a **MIMIC-IV-sourced plasmode with Indian-anchored graded shift and positivity diagnostics at each grade** — is absent.

Surviving falsifiable design: *Generate MIMIC-IV-derived plasmode cohort (n=20k encounters, MIMIC-IV as real covariate source, then overlay known outcome mechanism per Franklin 2014 plasmode), then re-weight / resample / outcome-generate toward CARRS- and ICMR-INDIAB-anchored targets under graded shift (G0–G3), compare doubly-robust / IOPW vs outcome-model transport estimators on bias, coverage, and positivity diagnostics (SMD >0.1 rate, overlap weight mass, S-score AUC, trimming fraction at α=0.1). Negative answer — that standard estimators already handle the shift (overlap unchanged, coverage nominal) — is publishable as "Indian-typical shift does not stress positivity for this estimand."*

### Evidence AGAINST the gap (closest defeater and why it does not close)

**Closest defeater(s):**

1. **Sri Lanka Framingham recalibration (Rannan-Eliya et al., BMC Public Health 2023, DOI 10.1186/s12889-023-17601-8, VERIFIED Cycle 1)** + Lancet Reg Health SE Asia hypertension control meta-analysis — these South Asian papers show *recalibration* suffices for Framingham-style scores in Sri Lankan/Indian populations, arguing formal transportability correction is decoration if recalibration is enough. **Why not a defeat:** They address *prediction model recalibration*, not **causal transportability positivity / visit-process informative missingness**; they neither inject covariate nor measurement-frequency shift in simulation, nor report transport weight diagnostics. The gap is precisely about diagnosing *when* recalibration fails due to positivity violation — which these papers assume away.
2. **GERAS / CARRS-based risk prediction models with South Asian validation (e.g., Nadkarni / CARRS-South Asian risk scores)** — some CARRS-derived models have been internally validated. **Why not a defeat:** Internal validation ≠ transport simulation; none reports graded MIMIC→CARRS shift injection with positivity diagnostics.
3. **Plasmode simulation literature (Franklin 2014 10.1093/aje/kww098 + Schneider 2025 simulation guidelines PMC12070788 + Liang arXiv 2410.13113 three-process joint) plus PlasmodeSim tutorials (EhsanX / Oxford Evans)** — robust simulation frameworks exist. **Why not a defeat:** None anchors shift magnitudes to ICMR-INDIAB MONO thin-fat distribution or Indian audit visit-process numbers; all published simulations condition on US covariate support.
4. **Termination condition if defeater materialises:** If a paper is located that reports a MIMIC-IV→South-Asian-anchored plasmode with MONO-calibrated BMI/WC joint distribution and selective-ordering missingness at ≥2 shift grades with SMD/overlap reporting, the gap converts to a replication/extension (e.g., add cost-driven switching + AYUSH + graded trimming sensitivity) rather than a de novo design.

*Implementation note:* Inject shifts sequentially per grade via importance re-weighting / outcome-mechanism stratification: e.g., for covariate shift, draw target pseudo-population by **propensity-tilting resampling** where log-odds of target membership = β₁·BMI + β₂·I(WC>threshold) + β₃·I(low HDL) + β₄·age-at-event, with β calibrated so weighted target marginals match ICMR-INDIAB prevalences (entropy balancing or iterative proportional fitting). For visit-process shift, delete labs/events with probability 1 − 1/(1+exp(−α·S_visit(X,cost))) where S_visit is a selection score for observation; α graded so marginal observation rates hit G1–G3 targets. Pre-register whether to **condition on full X including labs** (MAR under selective observation) vs **not conditioning on intermittently observed labs** (MNAR) — the latter stresses S-admissibility more.

#### What the graded design buys

* **Dose-response curve for diagnostics:** At what shift dose does overlap deteriorate (selection AUC >0.85, trimming fraction >20%, ESS <50% of sample)? If G2 still yields AUC ~0.62 and SMD>0.1 at ~15% covariates, standard IOPW remains viable and Indian shift is recalibration-addressable — a **publishable negative result**. If G2 yields AUC>0.80, trimming >30% needed, overlap weights re-center estimand meaningfully — transport correction is required.
* **Component decomposition:** Abate one shift dimension at a time (BMI alone vs WC/HDL added vs visit-process added) to isolate which channel drives positivity failure — thin-fat alone vs documentation/time-zero alone.
* **Sensitivity to cost-driven switching:** Treat switching as treatment-version violation (same "prescribed" ≠ same "dispensed/consumed"); report per-protocol vs intent rates across grades.

### Methodological implications

1. **Plasmode fragility is addressed by two-generative-framework sensitivity (T1 lesson).** Liu et al. 2025 plasmode cautionary (arXiv 2504.11740) shows Generate-Treatment vs Generate-Outcome plasmode have different guarantees; schedule **both**: (a) Generate-Outcome (fix X, generate Y under Indian-anchored mechanism) and (b) Generate-Treatment (fix X,Y, generate A under cost/AYUSH selection) and report whether conclusions about required correction are framework-dependent.
2. **Positivity diagnostics as primary outcomes, not afterthoughts.** Pre-register SMD histogram, S-score overlap plot, overlap-weight distribution, Li et al. 2018 overlap-weight ATO vs ATE comparison, Crump trimming at α=0.10, and Austin 2009 SMD>0.1 rule. Kang review shows these are rarely reported — the plasmode makes them the **primary estimands**; bias/coverage are secondary conditional on diagnostics.
3. **Standard estimators to compare:** IOPW (Dahabreh), outcome-model standardization (g-formula), doubly-robust augmented IOPW, and calibration weighting (Josey PMC10201931 calibration approach) — calibration may be more stable under support thinning than weighting; the graded shift identifies the crossover point.
4. **Sample-size / power for diagnostics:** Transport weight variance ∝ 1/(P(S=1|X) × P(S=0|X)); at G3 (high AUC), ESS collapses even at n=20k; plan bootstrap feasibility sweep (n=5k/10k/20k/50k) for diagnostics stability — mirrors Schneider simulation-guideline logic (vary frequency/noise/heterogeneity) but here varying shift dose.

### Clinical implications

* If thin-fat MONO alone stresses positivity, eligibility / screening criteria that gate on BMI≥25 will systematically exclude the Indian target’s highest-risk subpopulation (43% of adults invisible) — a **clinical equity failure** masked as methodological assumption violation. Plasmode quantifies the exclusion under BMI-gated trials.
* If visit-process shift alone dominates, the failure is not prediction-model miscalibration but **informative missingness / time-zero slippage** — interventions target workflow (structured documentation, lab stewardship) rather than algorithm choice. G3 night-shift injection OR 2.78 and diagnosis 8.5% suggest deployment-time clinical decision support would behave differently than trained-time MIMIC protocol.
* If AYUSH / cost-switching shift dominates bias, Indian deployment requires **formulary-aware and AYUSH-aware target-trial emulation** with treatment-version sensitivity (same prescription ≠ same exposure) — the design justifies an Indian-specific protocol rather than geographic replication.

### India relevance

**Verdict: `STRESSES-ASSUMPTION` — justified.**

This is not `GEOGRAPHY-ONLY` replication. The Indian setting stresses at least four transportability assumptions simultaneously and the magnitudes are now anchorable:

* **Positivity / overlap (primary):** Indian joint covariate support (BMI 21–24 ∩ metabolic syndrome) has near-zero mass in a BMI≥25-screened MIMIC-IV source. At G2–G3, P(S=1 | X) for the Indian metabolically obese phenotype is pushed toward 0, requiring weight extremes / trimming / ATO re-targeting. Standard transport estimators become unstable — a genuine positivity stress test, not merely lower mean BMI.
* **S-admissibility (selection diagram):** Selection S is influenced by cost, formulary availability, shift staffing, and AYUSH preference — none measured in MIMIC. If these S-nodes have a direct effect on Y (cost → outcome via persistence; AYUSH → herb-drug interaction → Y; night-shift staffing → outcome), S-admissibility fails even after conditioning on recorded X. The graded plasmode encodes the **untestable** direct S→Y edge as sensitivity (simulate unmeasured U for cost/AYUSH with audit-anchored prevalences 44–96% and ORs 1.5–2.5).
* **Consistency / treatment-version:** "Prescribed antihypertensive" fragments into branded vs Jan Aushadhi generic, single vs irrational FDC (80% of Indian-market FDCs irrational per Gov-ban literature cited in Khanna), IV vs oral (90% IV in ED) — consistency requires treatment well-defined; audit shows it is not.
* **Exchangeability / effect modification:** MONO carries T2D OR 6.90 and CAD OR 1.77 *at same BMI<25* — i.e., effect modification by underlying adiposopathy distribution that BMI does not capture. Transporting average treatment effect without interacting with metabolic-obesity indicators violates exchangeability.
* **Informative missingness / time-zero:** Documentation shift (diagnosis 8.5%, duration 9.6%) implies eligibility-defining variables are missing not at random conditional on outcome, violating the emulated trial’s time-zero assignment; this is a visit-process assumption failure specific to LMIC EHR workflows.

In short: the project turns "repeat US study on Indians" into **"which transport assumption fails first, at which shift dose, and can routine diagnostics detect it before deployment?"** — a methodological question where Indian epidemiology is the instrument, not the backdrop.
